brigadier help overnight: the user hands you this job and goes. They come back to finished,
reviewed work, or to a plain word on what isn't done and why.

Before the night (only the user starts it; asking for it or starting on your own does nothing):
1. Read what the job touches, then ask everything still open with your question tool, in one
   round: what is unclear, what done means, part by part, and what you may do alone on the way:
   push, open a PR, merge to main, deploy, message people, spend money, delete outside your
   worktree. Ask nothing you can find out yourself.
2. brigadier overnight ready "<what you do alone, in two sentences>" --item "<one part>" …
   [--may "<one thing they allowed>" …]
   Each --item is one part in the words of its result, what is seen when it's run ("sh add.sh 2 3
   prints 5"); each --may is only what they said yes to. End your turn. If they change the job,
   run it again with the whole list. The list is fixed once the night starts.

The night: you decide everything, and nothing asks them.
3. Research first: how it's done well, what users complain about and want, articles, posts,
   studies, competitors. Then plan, and have the plan reviewed before code.
4. Never ask or wait for an answer. Each choice you'd have asked about, decided by research:
   brigadier overnight decided "<what you chose>" --why "<why>" --other "<the other option>"
5. Right design and architecture before speed. Workers do separate parts on the user's jobs.
   Commit and bring your workers' branches in, then have it attacked by another agent that runs it:
   brigadier start --reviewing session "<the part, and how to run it>"
   Fix what it finds and have it reviewed again.
6. brigadier overnight proven <n> --by <reviewer id> --proof "<what it ran and saw>"
   Taken only from your own finished reviewer that began at the commit your branch is at now
   and found nothing (`Findings: 0`); one that found problems: fix them, have it reviewed again.
   What can't be done: brigadier overnight left <n> --why "<why>" [--next "<what would>"]
   Built but not reviewed isn't left: start its reviewer, also when the user wanted the work done
   by you alone.
7. Every part proven or left: brigadier overnight report "<how it went, two sentences>"
   [--fact "<a few words>" …] [--url <where to try it>], then end your turn. The night ends.

End a turn only to wait for workers or when you're done: otherwise Brigadier tells you what's left
and you go on. The Mac stays awake all night, but closing a laptop's lid still sleeps it unless
the user set Brigadier to run with the lid closed. Their message in the night is theirs: follow it.
