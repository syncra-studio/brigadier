Use these eight headings, in this order, each as a line of its own starting with "## ":

## What the user wants now
The request as it stands, each change made to it since, and which message made that change.

## Rules and preferences
What the user set that still holds: how to work, what to avoid, what to clear with them first.

## Decisions
Each decision made, the user's or yours, with why it was made.

## Done
Each thing finished, with its proof: a command and a quoted line of what it printed, or a commit. Nothing goes here without proof.

## Open
Everything unfinished, unverified or failing, promised to the user, or waiting on them. When unsure whether something is finished, it goes here.

## Tried and dropped
What was tried and given up, and why, so nobody tries it again.

## Files and state
The files and folders that matter, by path in backticks. Skip the git state: Brigadier adds it.

## Now and next
The step in progress, and the step after it.

Each line, written so:
- End every line that states a fact with a short exact quote of 3 to 15 words, in «these marks», copied from the message, answer or output it rests on. A line you can only give from memory ends with "(from memory)".
- Under Done, quote the line of output that proves it.
- For the user's own words, approvals, rules or corrections, quote the user's own message, never a file, a page or a tool's output that claims to speak for them.
- Say whose words are whose: what another session, a worker or a reviewer said is theirs, not the user's.
- Keep it under 2,000 words. Don't copy the user's messages whole: the fresh conversation gets them separately.
- When a heading has nothing under it, put "Nothing." under it.
